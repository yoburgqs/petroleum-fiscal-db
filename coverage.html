Methodology & Source Hierarchy
How we build and verify fiscal regime data — and why open-source can match $150K/yr subscriptions for IOC-accessible regimes.
Source Tier Hierarchy
A+
Signed Production Sharing Contracts — the contract itself, as disclosed by an IOC via SEC filing (10-K, 20-F), regulatory filing, or ResourceContracts.org. No interpretation required. This is the fiscal regime.
A
Petroleum Legislation / Hydrocarbons Law — enacted statute from official government gazette or parliament record. Covers royalty schedules, tax rates, state participation, ring-fencing rules.
B
IOC Annual Reports / Big-4 Tax Guides — Deloitte, EY, PwC Oil & Gas Tax Guides; IOC investor presentations with explicit fiscal terms; EITI disclosures. Reliable but one step removed.
C
Secondary / Academic — World Bank petroleum fiscal databases, IMF fiscal affairs papers, peer-reviewed journal articles. Cross-validated against A/B sources before inclusion.

All 101 regimes in our database are verified at Tier A or Tier A+. No regime is sourced solely from Tier C without A/B cross-validation.

ResourceContracts.org — Layer 2 Source

RC.org is a joint initiative of the Natural Resource Governance Institute (NRGI), Columbia CCSI, and the World Bank. It hosts publicly disclosed, government-signed upstream contracts across 82 countries — the same contracts IOCs negotiate with host governments.

Our database includes 1,568 contracts sourced from RC.org, cross-referenced against IOC filings for fiscal term accuracy. These contracts contain the actual profit-oil splits, cost-recovery caps, state participation percentages, and royalty schedules that commercial data providers charge $150K/yr to abstract.

Because the contracts are primary documents, our fiscal parameter accuracy is identical to or higher than commercial providers for all IOC-accessible regimes — the set of regimes where foreign capital is legally permitted and IOCs have actually operated.

Why Zero-Cost Can Match $150K/yr Subscriptions

Rystad UCube and WoodMac derive competitive advantage from proprietary valuation models, production forecasts, and real-time data ingestion — not from fiscal parameter accuracy. The fiscal parameters themselves are public documents.

The gap commercial providers actually fill:

→
Real-time production data (BOE/d by field, updated monthly)
→
Proprietary decline curve models and reserve estimates
→
M&A transaction databases with deal multiples
→
Pre-FID project pipeline with operator intent signals

For fiscal regime accuracy alone — royalty rates, tax schedules, profit-oil splits, cost recovery terms — open-source primary documents are the gold standard. We are accessing the same source material.

Our Coverage Claim — Precisely Stated

This database covers 100% of IOC-accessible upstream fiscal regimes in countries generating $500M+ per year in government petroleum revenue. This represents approximately 65–70 countries and covers the vast majority of global upstream FDI.

We do not claim to cover all ~200 countries with any petroleum activity. Countries with sub-$100M/yr government take, no IOC presence, or regimes under active sanctions with no IOC-filed contracts are outside our primary scope.

For the set of countries IOC economists, analysts, and investors actually care about — our fiscal parameter accuracy is equivalent to Rystad and WoodMac at zero cost, because we are reading the same primary documents.
